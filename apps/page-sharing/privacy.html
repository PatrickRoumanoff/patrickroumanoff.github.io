Privacy Policy — Page Sharing for Confluence

Effective date: 25 June 2026
Applies to: the Page Sharing for Confluence app for Confluence Cloud, published on the Atlassian Marketplace by Patrick Roumanoff trading as Cloudscript.io (ABN 72 287 293 373) ("CloudScript.io", "we", "us").

This policy explains what the Page Sharing for Confluence app does with data when it runs inside your Confluence Cloud site. Unlike a purely client-side app, this app stores some data and deliberately moves selected page content between Confluence sites, so this policy describes those flows in detail. The wording reflects the permissions the app actually requests, the data it actually stores, and the way content travels between a sharing site and a subscribing site.

Summary

Page Sharing for Confluence lets an administrator on one Confluence site (the "producer") deliberately share specific pages with named partner sites (the "consumers"), and lets those partner sites view a read-only copy of each shared page. The app runs entirely on Atlassian's Forge platform, within Atlassian's hosted environment; it does not send data to any CloudScript.io server, because CloudScript.io operates no server for this app, and it uses no analytics, cookies, or trackers. The app does, however, store a small amount of data in Atlassian's own storage on each site (including the Atlassian account identifiers of the administrators who operate it), and it copies the text of pages you choose to share into the partner sites you choose to share them with. Because the app stores personal data and moves content between sites, our Atlassian Marketplace declaration records that the app does store personal data.

What the app is

The app ships as a single Forge app that performs two roles. On a producer site, an administrator approves an individual page for external sharing, maintains an allow-list of the partner Confluence sites permitted to subscribe (identified by each site's Atlassian cloudId, a stable tenant identifier), and can stop sharing at any time. On a consumer site, the app subscribes to an approved page using an unguessable "magic link" provided by the producer, receives the page's content, stores a local copy, and displays it read-only on a dedicated Confluence page. The producer is in control throughout: a consumer only ever receives content the producer has both approved for the page and allow-listed for that consumer's site.

Where processing happens

All processing happens inside Atlassian's Forge environment. The producer reads the chosen page's body, reduces it to native content (described below), and sends it directly to the subscribing site's Forge endpoint; the consumer stores and renders that content within its own Forge environment. The only external destinations the app is permitted to contact are Atlassian-hosted Forge web triggers on the *.atlassian-dev.net domain, which is how one Confluence site's installation of the app talks to another's. The app declares no other external destinations, and on Forge an app can only contact destinations it has declared in advance, so the app cannot transmit content to CloudScript.io or to any unrelated third party.

What the app can access

On the producer side the app requests read access to page content (read:page:confluence) so it can read the body of a page approved for sharing, and a content-summary read permission (read:confluence-content.summary) used by the page-update mechanism that triggers a push when a shared page changes. It requests read access to user information (read:confluence-user) for a single purpose, to check whether the person performing a sharing action belongs to an administrator group, so that only administrators can approve sharing, manage the allow-list, or mint links; the result of that check is used at the moment of the action and is not stored. The app also requests app storage (storage:app) to keep the small amount of operational data described in the next section. The app does not request permission to modify or delete your pages, and it never writes content back from a consumer site to a producer site.

What data the app stores

The app stores data in Atlassian's Forge key-value storage, separately on each site, and only within that site's own storage namespace. On a producer site it stores, for each shared page, the sharing state and the page title together with the Atlassian account identifier of the administrator who approved it; the allow-list of permitted partner sites, each recorded as a cloudId with an optional human-readable label and the account identifier of the administrator who added it; the magic-link tokens that have been issued, each with the account identifier of the administrator who created it; and, for each active subscription, the partner site's cloudId, the partner site's receive endpoint, and a per-subscription secret used to authenticate pushes to that subscriber. On a consumer site it stores, for each subscription, a reference to the producer endpoint it enrolled with, the page title, the matching per-subscription secret, and a stored copy ("replica") of the page content it has received. The Atlassian account identifiers of the administrators who operate the app are the principal item of personal data the app holds on its own behalf; the page content it stores may contain further personal data depending on what your pages contain, as explained below.

Cross-site content sharing and your responsibilities

The core function of this app is to copy the text of a page from a producer site to one or more consumer sites that the producer has chosen and allow-listed. When an administrator approves a page and a partner site subscribes, and whenever that page is subsequently updated, the producer reads the page body, reduces it to a safe native-content-only form (it removes macros and extensions, attachments, and images and other embedded media, keeping headings, paragraphs, lists, tables, basic text formatting, and ordinary web and email links), and sends that reduced content to each eligible subscriber, where it is stored and displayed read-only. The consumer independently re-checks the received content against the same allow-list of permitted elements before displaying it, and discards anything it does not recognise. Because the producing organisation decides which pages to share and with which partner sites, that organisation is the controller of the page content and is responsible for ensuring it is appropriate to share that content (including any personal data it contains) with the recipient sites; CloudScript.io processes that content only as the mechanism the producer uses to share it, and does not select, inspect, or retain it on any CloudScript.io system.

How sharing is withdrawn, and what happens to stored copies

A stored copy on a consumer site persists until it is actively removed, and withdrawal is designed to remove it promptly. When a producer administrator stops sharing a page, removes a partner site from the allow-list, or revokes a subscription, the producer sends a delete instruction to the affected subscribers, and each consumer purges its stored copy of that page. If a delete instruction cannot be delivered immediately (for example, because a site is temporarily unreachable), delivery is retried, so a copy is not left indefinitely once sharing has been withdrawn. Uninstalling the app from a site removes the app's modules and discards the data the app held in that site's Forge storage, so there is no separate CloudScript.io system from which data would need to be deleted.

Personal data

The personal data the app itself stores is limited to the Atlassian account identifiers of the administrators who approve sharing, manage the allow-list, and issue links, which it stores so it can show who took those actions and operate the sharing controls. Separately, the page content that a producer chooses to share may contain personal data within the text of the page, and where it does, that content is copied to the subscribing sites the producer has allow-listed and stored there as a read-only copy until sharing is withdrawn. CloudScript.io does not collect names, email addresses, or other directory information into any CloudScript.io system, and operates no database, profile, or analytics store of its own for this app. Requests from individuals to access, correct, or delete personal data contained in shared page content should be directed to the organisation that operates the relevant Confluence site, since that organisation controls the page and decides what to share; CloudScript.io will assist a customer with such a request to the extent the app's controls allow (for example, by a producer withdrawing sharing so copies are purged).

How content and links are kept secure

Sharing is restricted to administrators, and the check for administrator status fails closed, so an action proceeds only when the app can positively confirm the person is an administrator. Subscribing to a page requires possession of an unguessable magic link, which is the deliberately shared capability that lets a partner site enrol. The producer enforces its allow-list on its own outbound side and re-checks, before every push, that the page is still shared, that the recipient site is still allow-listed, and that the subscription is still valid, skipping any recipient that fails. Each push to a consumer is authenticated by a per-subscription secret, and a consumer accepts a push only when it matches a subscription it created, so a site cannot be made to store content for a subscription it did not establish. Page content is reduced to native elements before it leaves the producer and re-validated before it is displayed on the consumer, as a deliberate double check that no active or unexpected content is carried across.

What the app does not do

The app does not send your content to CloudScript.io or to any external service other than the Atlassian-hosted Forge endpoints of the partner sites a producer has chosen, it stores no data on any CloudScript.io system because there is none, and it includes no analytics, advertising, cookies of its own, or third-party tracking code. It does not share macros, attachments, images, or other embedded media; it does not modify or delete your pages; and it provides no path for a consumer site to write content back to a producer site.

Atlassian Forge and the underlying platform

Because the app runs on Atlassian Forge, the platform itself is operated by Atlassian, and Atlassian's infrastructure, security model, and data residency arrangements govern how the app executes and where the data it stores in Forge storage resides. For information about how Atlassian handles data on Forge and within Confluence Cloud, including data residency, refer to Atlassian's own privacy and trust documentation. Your agreement with Atlassian governs the page content held within each Confluence site, including the read-only copies this app stores on a subscribing site.

Support

If you contact us for support at support@cloudscript.io, we receive the email address and any information you choose to include in your message, and we use it only to respond to and resolve your request. Support is handled by email; there is no support form that collects data on this website.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app changes what it accesses, stores, or transmits (for example, if a later release shares additional content types, introduces a CloudScript.io-hosted service, or adds analytics), this policy will be updated to describe that behaviour before that version is released, and the effective date above will change accordingly.
